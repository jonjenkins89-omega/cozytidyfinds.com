Henckels 14-Piece Self-Sharpening Knife Block: The Full Breakdown

Published August 2026 · 7 min read

Here's a confession: we owned dull knives for years and just accepted it. Sawing at a tomato until it surrendered. Pressing so hard on an onion that it shot across the cutting board. We knew the fix, in theory. Buy a sharpening stone, learn the angle, hone before every use like the serious cooking people do. We were never going to do any of that, and if you're honest with yourself, you're probably not going to either.

That's exactly why the self-sharpening knife block exists, and why the Henckels Graphite 14-piece set became the newest thing on our counter and the first thing we tell people about when they ask what actually changed in our kitchen. The pitch is almost unfairly simple: the block maintains the knives for you. Every time you slide a knife home, it gets a micro touch-up. No stone, no gadget, no willpower required.

How a Self-Sharpening Block Actually Works

The magic is in the slots. The block's storage slots for the fine-edged knives have built-in ceramic honing wheels. When you pull a knife out or slide it back in, the blade draws across those wheels at a consistent angle, and the edge gets realigned. That's honing: straightening the microscopic edge that folds over with normal use, which is what makes a knife feel dull long before the steel is actually worn.

Do that tiny correction twice per use, automatically, every single day, and the edge never gets the chance to degrade. That's the whole trick. It's not that the block does something extraordinary. It's that it does something ordinary with perfect consistency, which is precisely the thing humans are terrible at.

One honest note here: the serrated blades in the set don't ride the honing wheels. Serrated edges can't be honed that way, and they don't need to be. Their pointed teeth do the gripping work and keep cutting well for years by design. The self-sharpening slots are for the fine-edged knives, which are the ones that actually go dull on you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a self-sharpening knife block really work?
Yes, with one clarification: it hones rather than grinds. The ceramic wheels in the slots realign the edge every time a knife is stored or removed, which is the maintenance step most people skip. Because it happens automatically with every use, the knives stay at working sharpness for years instead of slowly going dull.
Do the serrated knives get sharpened too?
No, and they're not supposed to. Serrated edges can't be honed by wheels and keep cutting well for years on their own. The self-sharpening slots serve the fine-edged knives, which are the ones that lose their edge with daily use.
Can I put the knives in the dishwasher?
Please don't. Dishwasher detergent and heat are brutal on fine edges and handles. A ten-second hand wash and a towel dry is all these need, and it's the single best habit for making any good knife last decades.
Will I ever need to sharpen these knives myself?
Not for routine maintenance; the block handles that. Honing keeps an existing edge aligned, though, so after many years of heavy use a blade can eventually wear enough to want a true re-sharpening. That's a once-in-a-blue-moon errand, not a weekly chore.
Is a knife block better than a magnetic strip?
They solve different problems. A magnetic strip saves counter space and shows off your knives, but it does nothing for the edges. A self-sharpening block trades a corner of counter for knives that maintain themselves. If you weren't going to hone by hand anyway, the block quietly wins.
