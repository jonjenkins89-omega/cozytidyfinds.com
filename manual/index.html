1. How This Works (The One-Page Summary)

Here's the entire business model in plain English:

  1. You post products on social media. Short videos, photos, quick recommendations.
  2. People click your link. They go to Amazon.
  3. They buy stuff. You earn a commission on everything they purchase.
  4. You repeat. The more you post, the more you earn.

That's it. No inventory. No shipping. No customer service. You just connect people with products they already want to buy.

The critical thing to understand: this compounds over time. A post you make today can drive sales for months (especially on Pinterest). Every piece of content you create is a tiny revenue stream that keeps flowing. After 6-12 months of consistent posting, you'll have hundreds of these little streams all adding up.

The people who make real money at this aren't smarter or luckier. They just didn't quit in month two.

2. How to Spot Products That Will Sell

Not every product is worth your time. The ones that sell from social media share specific traits. Run every product through these tests before you promote it:

The Impulse Price Test

$12-30 is the sweet spot. People buy at this price range without overthinking it. They see it, they want it, they tap. Above $30, people start comparison shopping. Below $12, the commission isn't worth your effort. The $15-25 range is where the magic lives.

The Transformation Test

Can you film a satisfying before/after? If yes, it will perform. A messy drawer becoming an organized drawer. A cluttered counter becoming a clean counter. A bare shelf becoming a styled shelf. Transformation content is the #1 performer in the home niche because people can instantly imagine that result in their own space.

The Universal Pain Test

Does everyone have this problem? Messy drawers, tangled cords, cluttered counters, no storage space, ugly bathrooms. Universal problems are shareable problems. When someone watches your video and thinks "oh my god, that's MY drawer," they share it with their friend who has the same mess. Universal = viral potential.

The "Looks Expensive, Costs Little" Test

Does it make a space look upgraded for under $30? People love feeling like they got a deal. A $22 set of matching containers that makes a pantry look like it belongs in a magazine? That sells itself. The bigger the gap between perceived value and actual price, the better it performs.

The Buy-Multiples Test

Will people want 2-3 of these? Organizers, baskets, containers, shelf dividers. If someone needs one for the kitchen drawer, they probably need one for the bathroom drawer too. More items in cart = more commission from one click.

The Review Check

4.5+ stars AND 1,000+ reviews = proven product. Don't gamble on unproven items. If thousands of people love it, your audience probably will too. Low review counts mean returns, complaints, and damaged trust. Let other people test new products. You recommend proven winners.

3. How to Word Your Posts (Fill-in-the-Blank Templates)

You don't need to be a writer. You need a formula. Here are 8 templates that work. Fill in the blanks and post.

1. The Discovery Post

"This $[price range] Amazon find changed my [room]. I've been using it for [time] and [specific benefit]. Link in bio!"

2. The Before/After

"The [product] before and after. [Short reaction]. Link in bio for everything."

No long caption needed. The video does the talking.

3. The Roundup

"[Number] Amazon finds under $[amount] that make your [room] feel like [aspirational thing]"

Then list each item with a one-line benefit.

4. The Honest Review

"I bought the viral [product] so you don't have to. Verdict: [worth it/skip it]. [One specific thing that surprised you]. Link in bio."

5. The Routine

"My [morning/evening] reset routine. [List 3-4 actions mentioning products naturally]."

6. The FOMO

"The Amazon [product] my followers keep asking about. [Specific detail]. It's under $[range]. Link in bio before it sells out."

7. The Tip

"If your [space] looks cluttered, try [specific action with product]. Game changer."

8. The Question

"Is it just me or does everyone's [space] look like chaos? Here's what finally fixed mine."

Key Rules for Captions

  • Hook in the first line. People decide to keep reading in 1 second. Lead with something that makes them stop scrolling.
  • Keep it SHORT. 3-5 sentences max for most posts. Nobody reads paragraphs on social media.
  • Always end with a call to action. "Link in bio." "Comment LINK." "Tap the product." Tell them exactly what to do next.
  • Sound like you, not like an ad. Talk the way you'd talk to a friend showing them something cool you found.

4. What You Must Do (Non-Negotiable)

These aren't suggestions. These are the rules of the game. Skip any of these and the whole thing stalls.

  • Include #ad or #affiliate on EVERY post that links to a product. The FTC requires this. No exceptions. No "I forgot." It takes 2 seconds and protects you from fines.
  • Post consistently. Every single day. Even when it feels pointless. Even when nobody's liking your stuff yet. Growth comes from showing up. The algorithm rewards consistency more than quality.
  • Respond to every comment and DM. Engagement drives the algorithm. A post with 50 comments gets shown to 10x more people than a post with 2 comments. Every reply you write is free reach.
  • Update your link-in-bio page. Tell Jon which products to feature. It should always match what you're currently posting about. If you post about kitchen organizers, the link page should have kitchen organizers at the top.
  • Film everything in good natural light. Near a window. That's it. Bad lighting kills content no matter how good the product is. You don't need fancy equipment. You need a window and daytime.
  • Be honest. If something sucks, say so. If something is just okay, say that too. Trust is the only thing that matters long-term. One honest "skip this" review builds more trust than 50 glowing recommendations.

5. What You Must NOT Do

These will hurt you. Some will get the account shut down. Read this section carefully.
  • Never say "buy through my link" as a favor or guilt-trip. People buy when they genuinely want the product. The moment it feels like charity, they lose interest. Your job is to show them something they want, not ask for a favor.
  • Never post without disclosure (#ad or #affiliate). You can get fined by the FTC. It's not worth it. Ever.
  • Never buy products through your own affiliate link. Amazon catches this and terminates accounts. Not "might terminate." Will terminate. Don't even test it.
  • Never promise a specific price. Don't say "it's only $14.99!" Prices change constantly. Say "under $20" or "check the link for current price." If someone sees a different price than you promised, you lose trust.
  • Never copy another creator's video exactly. Study their format. Notice what works. But bring your own voice, your own space, your own personality. Copies always feel like copies.
  • Never buy followers. Fake followers don't buy products. The algorithm detects fake engagement and suppresses your reach. 500 real followers who trust you are worth more than 50,000 bots.
  • Never post 30 products at once. Feature 1-3 products per post. Go deep, not wide. Show the product in use. Show the transformation. One great product demo beats a rapid-fire list every time.
  • Never disappear for a week then flood with posts. Consistent beats intense. One post per day for 30 days destroys seven posts in one day followed by silence. The algorithm rewards patterns, not bursts.

8. The Hidden Actions That Compound

These are the things that feel like they don't matter but separate people who make money from people who quit after three months:

1. The 89-Day Cart Trick

When someone clicks your link and adds ANYTHING to their Amazon cart (even if they don't buy right away), you earn commission if they buy within 89 days. Your call-to-action should encourage "add to cart" not just "check it out." Say things like "add it to your cart before the price goes up" or "I'd grab it now, it goes in and out of stock."

2. The Everything-in-Cart Effect

When someone clicks your link, you earn commission on EVERYTHING they buy in that session, not just the product you linked. A $15 organizer click that leads to a $200 shopping spree = commission on all $200. This is why even "small" products are worth posting. You're not just selling that one item. You're opening a door to their entire cart.

3. Internal Linking on Pinterest

Every pin linking to the website (not directly to Amazon) builds the site's authority over time. More authority = higher Google rankings = free organic traffic forever. The website is your home base. Social media is how you drive people there.

4. Email List

Every email subscriber is someone you can reach anytime without an algorithm deciding if they see your content. Instagram could suppress your posts tomorrow. Your email list is yours forever. Even 100 subscribers who trust you is worth more than 10,000 followers who scroll past. Tell Jon to set up the weekly email when you're ready.

5. Content Batching

One 3-4 hour session per week creating all content for the next 7 days beats daily 30-minute scrambles. Quality goes up. Stress goes down. Pick one day, set up your filming area, shoot everything for the week, then schedule it all. Done.

6. Comment Engagement Loops

When someone comments "where did you get that?" reply with a video showing the product. This creates a new piece of content AND drives engagement on the original post AND gives you another chance to share the link. One comment becomes three wins.

7. Seasonal Front-Running

Post holiday/seasonal content 6-8 weeks BEFORE the event. Everyone else posts during the event. You'll already be ranking by then. January organization content should go up in late November. Back-to-school in June. Holiday gift guides in October. Early bird gets the traffic.

8. The "Everything They Buy" Audience

Target people who are in buying mode: new apartment, just moved, nesting, new homeowner, college dorm setup, first house. These people buy HUNDREDS of dollars of stuff after one click. They need everything. One click from a new homeowner is worth twenty clicks from someone who's just browsing.

Day 3-4: Your First Videos

  • Pick 5 products from the website that you genuinely like
  • Film a 15-30 second video for each one (just show the product, show it in use, keep it simple)
  • Post one per day for the next 5 days

Day 5-7: Pinterest Kickoff

  • Create 15 Pinterest pins from the products on the site

Week 2: Build the Rhythm

  • Post daily on Instagram (1 Reel) and TikTok (2 videos)
  • Keep creating Pinterest pins (aim for 10/day scheduled via Tailwind)
  • Start commenting on 20 accounts in your niche daily (this builds your visibility)

Week 3: Learn from Your Data

  • Look at your analytics. Which posts got the most views? Make more like those.
  • Which products got clicks? Feature those again from a different angle.
  • What time of day performed best? Post more at that time.

Week 4: Lock In the System

  • You should have a rhythm now
  • Content batching day once per week
  • Daily posting on schedule
  • Daily engagement (comments, DMs, community interaction)
  • This is the system from now on. It's not glamorous. It's effective.
